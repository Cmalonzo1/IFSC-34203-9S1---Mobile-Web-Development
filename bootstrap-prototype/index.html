Reflection

While I imagine most people come in thinking we’re focusing strictly on developing mobile web applications, it’s so much more. It’s developing websites for all devices. Mobile Web Development expanded on many of the topics covered in Web Technologies, including various tags, elements, while introducing us to new topics, like web frameworks, dynamic websites through media queries, and especially gridbox. I cannot appreciate gridbox enough. Once you’ve learned and understood its syntax, it simplifies positioning elements to a substantial degree, and flexbox will only be used in very specific circumstances.

This course was paced very well, and, in Thomas Wallace fashion, the lectures were very interactive. We all appreciate his eagerness for the success of his students. They are actively engaged with the material at all times. It’s not just writing notes down, it’s building something from the ground up in real time. The focus on experiential learning highlights key points in the lecture and helps students tremendously remembering functionality, use cases, and syntax. Additionally, the assignments aren’t overly challenging to keep you in your chairs for days on one assignment. Rather, they’re simple, but packed with content, ensuring every part of the topic is covered.

The final assignment was a major step up from the final in Web Technologies. It was similar, but we had to consider other devices in the mix; not just ours. Wizard101 is still one of my favorite games out there. However, they’ve only stepped up their website with the release of the two latest worlds. They decided to give these two worlds standalone pages, rather than create a single landing page that answers these questions: “What’s the game? What can I do? Where can I download it?” This was the idea I had when I created my final. It’s still very messy, but the lack of official images and a home website dating back to the early 2000s with a billion separate pages made this finally incredibly difficult to visualize something that would fit Wizard101’s ‘vibe’. I made this known to the people I shared this project with, and they agreed. It looks off. And quite frankly, this probably wasn’t the right project to perform a mobile app for. This game isn’t even on mobile 😭.

Overall, I’m satisfied with the course. I refreshed my mind and learned a lot as well. I think I’ll stick with plain CSS, though. Tailwind can be incredibly cluttered with all the pre-made classes, and I’ll be looking through the documentation more than VSCode. And Bootstrap imprisons your creativity.
